## Video 2 — Emma from mmmEnglish

### Communication and conversation

#### 1. bring up
- **Meaning:** Introduce or start discussing a topic, usually something serious or important.
- **Context:** Often used in professional or serious conversations.
- **Example:** “Let's bring this up at our next team meeting.”

#### 2. get across
- **Meaning:** Communicate a message so that someone understands it.
- **Common objects:** facts, feelings, ideas, a message, meaning, a point, a point of view.
- **Example:** “Am I getting the message across clearly?”
- **Example:** “I'm trying to get my point across but Paul keeps butting in.”

#### 3. jump in
- **Meaning:** Interrupt or join a conversation.
- **Register:** Informal.
- **Example:** “Do you mind if I jump in here? I've got something to share.”

#### 4. butt in
- **Meaning:** Join a conversation or activity without being asked or invited to.
- **Register:** Very informal and potentially rude; suggests selfish interruption.
- **Example:** “I was speaking with Sue ... and then Jim butts in and starts talking about his new promotion.”

#### 5. blurt out
- **Meaning:** Say something without thinking about its effect, often because of nervousness or excitement.
- **Example:** “I just blurted it out. I didn't think.”

#### 6. get through
- **Meaning 1:** Successfully reach someone by phone when they answer.
- **Example:** “I'm trying to get through but there's no answer.”
- **Meaning 2:** Make someone understand what you are trying to say.
- **Example:** “It's difficult to get the message through to my team.”

#### 7. talk over
- **Meaning 1:** Discuss a problem or plan in order to clarify it, find a solution, or resolve it.
- **Grammar:** Requires an object, such as “an issue,” “a plan,” “something,” “it,” or “this.”
- **Example:** “Let's talk it over tonight when you get home.”
- **Meaning 2:** Speak over someone, often interrupting them.
- **Grammar:** In this use, it is followed by the person being interrupted.
- **Example:** “I don't mean to talk over you but we've already decided what to do.”

#### 8. talk around / talk someone round
- **Meaning:** Convince or persuade someone to agree with you.
- **Example:** “I didn't want to go but he succeeded in talking me round.”

#### 9. shut down
- **Meaning 1:** Close something, usually permanently, such as a restaurant.
- **Meaning 2:** Reject or dismiss someone or their idea during a conversation.
- **Register:** The conversational meaning is negative.
- **Example:** “She just shut him down mid-sentence.”
- **Example:** “My boss didn't like the suggestion. He shut me down straight away.”

#### 10. back up
- **Meaning:** Give support by agreeing with or defending someone or something.
- **Example:** “Thanks for backing me up during the meeting.”

### Looking and investigating

#### 11. look back on
- **Meaning:** Think about or reflect on an event, time, or experience in the past.
- **Example:** “I try to look back on the mistakes I made in the past and learn from them.”

#### 12. look after
- **Meaning:** Take care of someone or something.
- **Example:** “I offered to look after my sister's kids on Thursday night.”

#### 13. look down on
- **Meaning:** Have a low opinion of someone or think you are better than them.
- **Example:** “It's really common for people who value university education to look down on those who don't have a degree.”

#### 14. look for
- **Meaning:** Try to find something.
- **Grammar:** Requires a noun or object explaining what is being sought.
- **Example:** “I'm looking for my keys, I can't find them anywhere.”

#### 15. look forward to
- **Meaning:** Anticipate something with excitement or pleasure.
- **Example:** “I'm looking forward to summer.”

#### 16. look around / look round
- **Meaning:** Visit or inspect a place to see what is there.
- **Example:** “Before I book the venue, I'd like to come and look around.”

#### 17. look out
- **Meaning 1:** An exclamation used to warn someone to be careful.
- **Example:** “Look out! You're about to knock the glass off the bench!”
- **Meaning 2: look out for someone:** Take care of someone and make sure they are okay.
- **Example:** “My nephews are always looking out for each other at school.”

#### 18. look up
- **Meaning 1:** Search for information, often using a dictionary, Google, or YouTube.
- **Grammar:** Separable in this meaning.
- **Examples:** “Look up the word.” / “Look the word up.”
- **Meaning 2: look up to someone:** Respect or admire someone and want to be like them.
- **Grammar:** Inseparable in this meaning; the object follows the phrasal verb.
- **Example:** “I really look up to my boss.”

#### 19. look into
- **Meaning:** Investigate something.
- **Context:** Particularly useful in professional situations.
- **Example:** “Maybe I'll look into it and report back after lunch.”

#### 20. look over
- **Meaning:** Examine or review something quickly, without going into a lot of detail.
- **Example:** “I'll look over the report tonight.”

#### 21. look through
- **Meaning:** Examine or search through something, especially to find needed information.
- **Example:** “I can spend hours looking through recipes.”

### Health, money, and daily life

#### 22. come down with
- **Meaning:** Begin to show signs of an illness.
- **Typical use:** Common, non-serious illnesses such as a cold, flu, or stomach bug.
- **Grammar:** Transitive and inseparable; an object is required and follows the phrasal verb.
- **Example:** “They both came down with a terrible cold.”
- **Example:** “I feel like I'm coming down with something.”

#### 23. fight off
- **Meaning:** Overcome an illness by fighting against it; the “fighting” is figurative.
- **Grammar:** Transitive and separable.
- **Examples:** “She fought off the cold.” / “She fought the cold off.” / “She fought it off.”
- **Pronoun rule:** With a pronoun, the object goes between the verb and particle: “She fought it off,” not “She fought off it.”

#### 24. pass away
- **Meaning:** Die.
- **Register:** A softer, more polite, and respectful expression.
- **Grammar:** Intransitive and inseparable; it does not require an object.
- **Example:** “When I talk about my dad, I don't say he died, I say he passed away.”

#### 25. dip into
- **Meaning:** Spend some money that has been saved for a specific purpose.
- **Typical objects:** savings, a pension, a retirement fund.
- **Example:** “They have dipped into their savings to pay for their renovation.”

#### 26. get by
- **Meaning:** Manage to live or complete a task using only the money or knowledge currently available.
- **Example:** “Even though Tim has been without work for six months they've been getting by.”

#### 27. go without
- **Meaning:** Live without something that is needed or desired.
- **Example:** “They went without power for five days.”
